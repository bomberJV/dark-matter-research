WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND

Five things to try for yourself.

  1. The halo moves the answer more than the physics. Load with their cross-section fixed, then switch between "This paper's halo" and "Common". The same particle goes from about 1.4 selected events to about 67.
  2. A factor of 4 in the higgsino cross-section. Two conventions are in print, 7.4 × 10⁻³⁹ and 1.86 × 10⁻³⁹ cm². Retuned to one event, the larger one predicts about 4.5 sideband events, the smaller about 2.1: a 1% versus 12% chance that the sideband stays empty.
  3. The sideband splits the field in two. Splittings near 360–380 keV with electroweak strength predict 1.2–5.8 sideband events per signal event; everything else predicts under 0.5. Raise the escape speed to 600 km/s on and watch that number collapse.
  4. One event barely discriminates. Across all 24 benchmarks the fit penalty runs from 1.7 to 4.9: at most a factor of about 12 in likelihood.
  5. The date counts. The event came on June 16, close to Earth's fastest point through the halo. Endothermic models expect up to 3.9 times their average rate then; exothermic ones barely change. Set the time to June 16 and December 2 to see it.

Limits

Helm nuclear form factor only (its second zero sits at 279 keV, where shell-model responses differ); LZ's efficiency approximated as a piecewise-linear curve; sideband acceptance assumed complete because LZ has not published it; backgrounds neglected (about 0.01 events expected near 248 keV); contact spin-independent couplings only, so boosted dark matter and momentum-dependent interactions are not covered. Benchmarks were read from each paper by an AI reader and checked where a reproduction was possible. See the full write-up.
